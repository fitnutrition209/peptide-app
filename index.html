Peptides in plain words

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids — the same building blocks that make up proteins, just fewer of them. Your body makes thousands of peptides that act as messengers (insulin is a famous one). Drug makers copy or tweak them to switch specific signals on or off.

Why the buzz?

GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide and tirzepatide showed large weight loss in big trials, pulling attention to the whole category — including unproven "wellness" peptides.

💉

Usually injected

Most peptides break down in the gut, so many are injected under the skin. Exceptions include collagen powders and topical skin products.

⚖️

"Natural" ≠ proven

Many trending peptides have impressive animal studies but little or no controlled human data. That gap is where most of the hype lives.

Four ways peptides reach people in the US

1. FDA-approved prescriptionReviewed for safety, effectiveness and manufacturing quality (e.g., Wegovy, Zepbound, Vyleesi).
2. Compounding pharmaciesCustom-made, not FDA-approved. Legal only within limits; FDA tightened rules on GLP-1 copies in 2025–2026.4,6
3. Gray-market "research use only"Online vials labeled "not for human use." FDA says that label doesn't protect sellers whose sites imply human use.12,13
4. Supplements & cosmeticsCollagen powders, copper-peptide serums. FDA doesn't approve supplements before they're sold.27

"FDA-approved" applies only to the specific approved product and use — not to every way people use that peptide.

Dosing on each card is educational only, not a dosing guide: FDA label doses for approved products, doses studied in trials for investigational drugs, and a plain "no established dose" note for research peptides without tested human doses. Where researchcfps.com lists a community protocol, it appears in a separate, clearly labeled box: community-reported, not tested or approved. Talk to a licensed clinician.

Why purity matters

Research-market and illegally sold peptides are not the same as FDA-approved pharmacy products. Independent labs (for example Finnrick's public HPLC testing) repeatedly find vials that fail identity, fall short of claimed purity, or miss the labeled fill amount. Peer-reviewed work on gray-market semaglutide has also found low measured purity, overfill, and endotoxin. Contaminants (endotoxin, heavy metals, wrong peptide, residual solvents) can cause fever, infection risk, organ injury or simply mean you are not getting the molecule on the label. A vendor Certificate of Analysis is not a substitute for independent testing. FDA-approved drugs are made under GMP with lot release testing — that pathway does not apply to research vials sold online.

Storage & reconstitution (general)

For FDA-approved products, follow the label (usually refrigerate pens/vials, do not freeze, protect from light, and observe in-use dating). For lyophilized research peptides, FDA CMC guidance notes dry powder is more stable when kept cold, dry and protected from light; once in solution, degradation accelerates. Use sterile diluent and technique; do not invent beyond-use dates. This app does not list community storage recipes unless a cited source provides them — researchcfps.com pages checked for this update did not include a storage specification beyond a reconstitution calculator link.
