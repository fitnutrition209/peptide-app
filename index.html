Peptides in plain words

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids — the same building blocks that make up proteins, just fewer of them. Your body makes thousands of peptides that act as messengers (insulin is a famous one). Drug makers copy or tweak them to switch specific signals on or off.

🧬

Why the buzz?

GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide and tirzepatide showed large weight loss in big trials, pulling attention to the whole category — including unproven "wellness" peptides.

💉

Usually injected

Most peptides break down in the gut, so many are injected under the skin. Exceptions include collagen powders and topical skin products.

⚖️

"Natural" ≠ proven

Many trending peptides have impressive animal studies but little or no controlled human data. That gap is where most of the hype lives.

Four ways peptides reach people in the US

1. FDA-approved prescriptionReviewed for safety, effectiveness and manufacturing quality (e.g., Wegovy, Zepbound, Vyleesi).Tap to learn more

Approved drugs went through clinical trials and an FDA review of safety, effectiveness and manufacturing before sale. Each lot is made under GMP rules, and the label lists doses, side effects and warnings.

Approval doesn't mean risk-free: FDA warns that counterfeit Ozempic has turned up in the U.S. supply chain, so buy only from licensed pharmacies with a prescription.180

2. Compounding pharmaciesCustom-made, not FDA-approved. Legal only within limits; FDA tightened rules on GLP-1 copies in 2025–2026.4,6Tap to learn more

Compounding pharmacies prepare custom versions of a drug for a specific patient with a prescription. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, so FDA has not reviewed their safety, effectiveness or quality before use.8

Pharmacies may only use bulk ingredients that meet legal criteria. FDA keeps a list of substances, including several peptides, that it says may present significant safety risks in compounding.33 Rules for GLP-1 copies tightened as shortages ended.4,6

3. Research-use-only (RUO) peptidesSold online for laboratory research and labeled "not for human use." Quality varies widely by supplier. FDA has warned sellers whose sites imply human use.12,13Tap to learn more

Not all research peptides are created equal. Quality varies widely from supplier to supplier, and the label alone can't tell you which kind of vial you have. RUO peptides aren't reviewed by a regulator before sale, so independent, batch-specific testing is how a supplier shows its quality. FDA has sent warning letters to sellers whose sites imply human use.12,13

Why testing matters: in one peer-reviewed test purchase, researchers analyzed semaglutide vials bought online by LC-MS. Measured purity was only 7.7%–14.37% versus the 99% on the label, drug content was 28.56%–38.69% above the labeled amount, and endotoxin (a bacterial toxin that can cause fever and inflammation) was found in all samples. Half of the six orders were never delivered (scams).226,227 Results like these are why a real, batch-matched COA matters.

What a credible certificate of analysis (COA) shows:

  • A lot/batch number that matches the number printed on your vial
  • The name of an independent, third-party testing lab, plus the test date
  • Purity % by HPLC
  • Identity confirmed by mass spectrometry (measured mass matches the peptide)
  • Contamination testing such as endotoxin and sterility results

Red flags: one generic COA reused for every batch, no lot number, an old or undated report, in-house testing only, a lab you can't look up, or a seller that won't share a COA. A good COA is evidence about the batch that was tested; it doesn't make a product FDA-approved or tested for human use.

4. Supplements & cosmeticsCollagen powders, copper-peptide serums. FDA doesn't approve supplements before they're sold.27Tap to learn more

Collagen powders are sold as dietary supplements and copper-peptide serums as cosmetics. FDA does not approve supplements before they are sold; makers are responsible for safety and labeling.27 Cosmetics are FDA-regulated but not FDA-approved, and they may not claim to treat disease.95

Oral collagen has randomized-trial evidence for skin hydration and elasticity, though results vary by study quality.25,26

Dosing on each card is educational only, not a dosing guide: FDA label doses for approved products, doses studied in trials for investigational drugs, and a plain "no established dose" note for research peptides without tested human doses. Where researchcfps.com lists a community protocol, it appears in a separate, clearly labeled box: community-reported, not tested or approved. Talk to a licensed clinician.

Why purity matters

Research-market and illegally sold peptides are not the same as FDA-approved pharmacy products. Independent labs (for example Finnrick's public HPLC testing) repeatedly find vials that fail identity, fall short of claimed purity, or miss the labeled fill amount. Peer-reviewed work on gray-market semaglutide has also found low measured purity, overfill, and endotoxin. Contaminants (endotoxin, heavy metals, wrong peptide, residual solvents) can cause fever, infection risk, organ injury or simply mean you are not getting the molecule on the label. A vendor Certificate of Analysis is not a substitute for independent testing. FDA-approved drugs are made under GMP with lot release testing — that pathway does not apply to research vials sold online.

Storage & reconstitution (general)

For FDA-approved products, follow the label (usually refrigerate pens/vials, do not freeze, protect from light, and observe in-use dating). For lyophilized research peptides, FDA CMC guidance notes dry powder is more stable when kept cold, dry and protected from light; once in solution, degradation accelerates. Use sterile diluent and technique; do not invent beyond-use dates. This app does not list community storage recipes unless a cited source provides them — researchcfps.com pages checked for this update did not include a storage specification beyond a reconstitution calculator link.

      What does "research use only" (RUO) mean?Tap to learn more

      It's a label for chemicals sold for laboratory research, not for use in people. It is not an FDA approval and not a quality grade; RUO products from different suppliers can differ a lot. FDA has said the label doesn't shield sellers whose websites imply human use, and in 2026 it sent warning letters calling RUO retatrutide and tirzepatide unapproved new drugs.12,13,14 More in the four-ways cards in Basics.

      Are research peptides legal to buy?Tap to learn more

      RUO peptides are widely sold online. FDA's 2026 enforcement has focused on sellers that market them for human use, through warning letters.12,13,14 The rules depend on the product and how it is marketed, and they are changing: an FDA "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" has been under White House review since Oct 14, 2026 and is not yet in effect.187 This app is not legal advice.

      Are all research peptides the same quality?Tap to learn more

      No. Quality varies widely between suppliers and even between batches. Independent testing is the way to tell them apart. In one published test purchase of online semaglutide, measured purity was far below the label and endotoxin was present, which shows why batch testing matters.226,227 See card 3 in the four-ways cards in Basics.

      How can I check a supplier's quality?Tap to learn more

      Ask for a batch-specific certificate of analysis (COA) from an independent, third-party lab, and check that:

      • The lot number on the COA matches the lot number on your vial
      • The lab is named and can be looked up, and the report is dated
      • Purity % is measured by HPLC
      • Identity is confirmed by mass spectrometry
      • Endotoxin and sterility results are included
      What are the red flags on a COA?Tap to learn more

      The same generic COA shown for every batch, no lot number, an old or undated report, in-house testing only, a lab name you can't verify, or a seller that won't share a COA at all.

      What are HPLC and mass spec?Tap to learn more

      HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography) separates what's in a sample, which is how purity % is estimated. Mass spectrometry (MS) measures the molecule's mass to confirm it is the peptide on the label. Researchers used LC-MS, the two combined, to test online semaglutide.226

      What is endotoxin, and why test for it?Tap to learn more

      Endotoxin is a toxin from bacteria that can cause fever and inflammation if injected. "Sterile" and "endotoxin-free" are different tests: in the online semaglutide study, the samples had no living microbes, yet every one contained endotoxin.226 That's why a complete COA reports both.

      Why does "animal study" vs "human trial" matter?Tap to learn more

      Results in rats don't always carry over to people, and doses don't convert directly. Reviews of BPC-157, for example, note that most of its evidence comes from animal studies, with very limited human data.15,16 A randomized, controlled human trial is the strongest kind of evidence.

      What is BPC-157's FDA status?Tap to learn more

      BPC-157 is not FDA-approved. FDA removed it from its Category 2 compounding list on Apr 22, 2026 after the nomination was withdrawn, which did not add it to the list pharmacies may compound.17,31 On July 23, 2026, FDA's advisory committee voted 8–6 to recommend adding it; the vote is advisory.18 As of Oct 9, 2026, FDA hadn't acted.19 It is also prohibited in sport by WADA.21 Details on the BPC-157 card.

      Semaglutide vs tirzepatide vs retatrutide: what's the difference?Tap to learn more
      • Semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) acts on one hormone receptor, GLP-1. FDA-approved.1
      • Tirzepatide (Zepbound, Mounjaro) acts on two, GLP-1 and GIP. FDA-approved. In a head-to-head trial it led to more weight loss than semaglutide (20.2% vs 13.7% at 72 weeks).3
      • Retatrutide acts on three: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. It is still investigational; Lilly plans to file for approval in Q1 2027.10,11

      See the semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide cards, or put them side by side in Compare.

      Compounded vs FDA-approved: what's the difference?Tap to learn more

      An approved product was reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness and manufacturing quality. A compounded drug is custom-made by a pharmacy for a patient with a prescription and is not FDA-approved.8 With the GLP-1 shortages over, FDA has limited compounded copies of semaglutide and tirzepatide,4,6 and it has warned about dosing errors with compounded semaglutide.9

      mg vs mcg: how do they convert?Tap to learn more

      1 mg (milligram) = 1,000 mcg (micrograms, also written µg). So 0.5 mg = 500 mcg, and 2.5 mg = 2,500 mcg. Mixing up the two is a 1,000-fold error, so always check which unit a number uses.

      How do "units" on an insulin syringe work?Tap to learn more

      On a U-100 syringe, 100 units = 1 mL, so 1 unit = 0.01 mL. The math, step by step:

      • Concentration = amount in the vial ÷ mL of water added
      • mL needed = amount wanted ÷ concentration
      • Units = mL × 100

      Example: 20 mg in 2 mL = 10 mg/mL (10,000 mcg/mL); 500 mcg ÷ 10,000 mcg/mL = 0.05 mL = 5 units. This is arithmetic only, not a dose recommendation.

      Does adding more water change the amount in the vial?Tap to learn more

      No. The total amount in the vial stays the same; more water only makes it more dilute. The same amount then takes more units to draw. For example, 500 mcg from a 20 mg vial is 5 units with 2 mL of water, 7.5 units with 3 mL, and 10 units with 4 mL.

      Are peptides allowed in competitive sports?Tap to learn more

      Many are not. The World Anti-Doping Agency's Prohibited List covers many peptides, including BPC-157 and growth-hormone-releasing peptides. Athletes in tested sports should check the current list.21

      Is "FDA-approved" the whole story?Tap to learn more

      Approval covers a specific product for a specific use; it doesn't cover every way people use that peptide. Many widely used products, like supplements, are never FDA-approved,27 and approved drugs have sometimes been withdrawn later.218,220 See Not FDA-Approved to weigh FDA status alongside the evidence.
